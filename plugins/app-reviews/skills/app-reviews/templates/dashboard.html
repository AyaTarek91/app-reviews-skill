What this channel cannot see

  • Usability. 145 reviews, and that is a floor. People muddle through and blame themselves. Settle it with drop-off by screen, not reviews.
  • Which device. Nobody writes "on Android 9". Use the store split instead — iOS 1.54 against Android 4.09.
  • What people want. Feature requests come only from those who stayed. Anyone who needed something missing and left is invisible.
  • A bug from a decision. The app is mobile-data-only by design. 253 reviews read as a defect and are not one.
  • Whether a charge was wrong. 831 reviews say a customer believes they were. Only billing records say if they were right.

Take these three to analytics

Open questions
Question 1

Did 2026.4.3 break something, or did the audience change?

Compare crash-free rate and funnel completion for 2026.4.2 users against 2026.4.3 users. Degraded funnels mean the release. Flat funnels mean the audience. Reviews cannot separate these — network complaints rose 28 points, and an app release cannot degrade a mobile network.

Question 2

How many people cannot log in at all?

17 reviews describe one deadlock: the password requires English digits to set, then types Arabic digits at login, so it can never match. Present since February, across ten versions — older than the release problem. Check login failure rate and repeat password resets.

Question 3

Is the money complaint real?

Billing one-star share went from 64% to 85% after the release — the highest figure in this dataset. Check unexpected-charge disputes and wallet transaction failures by app version.

How the areas were made

Method

Four steps

  1. Collected 34,461 reviews from both stores over six months. 7,929 had more than a few words; the rest were rating-prompt taps.
  2. A computer grouped them into 32 piles by shared words. It was given no word list and knows nothing about the app — it only sees which reviews sound alike.
  3. A person read all 32 and named them. Piles given the same name merged. About 8 were clean topics; the rest were emotion, vague complaints, or split by language.
  4. Then searched by keyword for each named area, to catch themes too small for a pile.

Why 32 piles for 7 areas

Deliberately more piles than areas. Merging two piles is typing the same name twice. Splitting one is impossible — so the setting is always too many, never too few.

Tested it: rerunning with only 10 piles did not remove the noise. It jammed login together with update, and balance with bundles. Fewer piles lose the topics, not the noise.

The keyword search exists because a pile needs about 240 reviews to survive. Accessibility had 4. Grouping alone would never have shown it.

Apple caps its feed at ~100 reviews, so App Store figures cover 26 Mar – 7 Aug only and are never merged with Play. Every count above is against the 7,929 written reviews, never the 34,461 total — roughly one review in nine describes anything at all.
